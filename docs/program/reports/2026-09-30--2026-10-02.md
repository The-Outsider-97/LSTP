## Problems discovered / self-review

The previous cycle’s stated next step — “resolve the evidence representation, then replace `octad_schema.json`” — was too optimistic. Re-inspection confirms that evidence cannot be resolved safely by implementation inference alone.

The subordinate specification currently accepts `provided`, `needed`, `assumptions`, and `challenges`, including string or structured evidence items. The authoritative reconciliation instead identifies user, sensor, model, tool, retrieved, and inferred provenance/source constructors. There is no demonstrated one-to-one, lossless mapping between those two models.

The correct engineering outcome is therefore not to choose one shape. It is to block canonical evidence certification until the authority is clarified.

## Security / reliability findings

Evidence is provenance-sensitive input. Heuristically translating evidence can incorrectly attribute support to a user, model, tool, sensor, retrieval source, or inference. Such a transformation could affect downstream trust, audit, and reasoning even though evidence does not itself grant authority.

D-014 therefore requires unmappable evidence to fail closed for canonical conformance. Namespaced preservation is acceptable only where the protocol explicitly permits extension preservation; it must not be relabeled as canonical evidence.

Permission behavior remains unchanged: packet permission data is requested authority only and cannot grant host authority.
